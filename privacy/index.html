The zero-exfiltration specification.

The formal data-protection protocol behind MomoTTS — written to be read, not buried. Version 1.0.0 · Effective August 21, 2026 · Architect: Ansari Bilal.

Abstract — the Zero-Exfiltration Guarantee. All neural acoustic synthesis, phonemization, text chunking, and master audio stitching execute strictly within the sandboxed local process memory of the host Android operating system. No user text, speech output, voice selection, or telemetry data is collected, stored, or transmitted over any external network. The app contains no analytics SDK, no advertising framework, and no crash-reporting beacon.

1 · Threat model & data flow

Cloud-hosted TTS APIs create an unavoidable surveillance surface: your words must travel to a server, be processed by someone else's hardware, and travel back. MomoTTS eliminates that surface by design rather than by promise. The pipeline respects three hard computational boundaries:

  • Text ingestion layer. Text provided to the Voice Studio or Dialogue Editor lives only in ephemeral memory owned by the app process. No remote logging, no keystroke analysis, no "anonymized" copy.
  • Inference execution layer. Neural graph execution runs exclusively on local hardware through compiled native ONNX runtimes. Model weights are read from sandboxed device storage; no sockets open.
  • Audio stitching layer. Generated PCM samples are compiled into WAV or MP3 containers on device, where they remain accessible only to you until you explicitly export or share them.

2 · Storage classification & permissions

MomoTTS limits internal storage strictly to operational requirements — every byte is itemized below.

MomoTTS requests no dangerous permissions — there is no storage-permission surface, no contacts, no location, no microphone. Recordings made of text you paste or type are synthesized speech, not captured audio of you.

3 · Telemetry, analytics & advertising

Zero. No behavioral analytics SDKs, no advertising identifiers, no crash reporters with network egress. The network interface is used for exactly one purpose: user-initiated retrieval of neural voice model weights from the Voice Hub. Once a model is cached locally, the radio stays idle during all synthesis — airplane mode is fully supported and functionally identical.

4 · Children's privacy (COPPA / GDPR)

Because MomoTTS collects zero personally identifiable information from any user, it operates in strict compliance with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and Article 8 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). The application is safe for users of all age brackets — there is simply no data to profile, sell, or leak.

5 · Your rights & data erasure

Under GDPR, CCPA, and global privacy frameworks you hold complete ownership of your data. Because every asset is local, exercising those rights is instant and does not require trusting anyone:

  • Right of access. All generated WAV and MP3 files are directly inspectable, playable, and exportable on the device — no request forms, no waiting periods.
  • Right to erasure. Purge temporary audio buffers instantly via Settings → Clear Temporary Audio Cache, or remove every project record by clearing app storage in Android system settings. Uninstalling removes all of it, permanently.

6 · Third-party engine attributions

The studio stands on the shoulders of open-source speech research. Inference is orchestrated by the Sherpa-ONNX runtime; voices are built on Piper VITS (MIT-licensed tooling and models) and the Kokoro-82M architecture (Apache 2.0). Their licenses are respected without modification, and no voice model is bundled without its upstream terms intact.

7 · Changes to this specification

If the architecture ever changes — it hasn't, and the roadmap says it won't — this document is versioned and dated at the top of the page. The app itself ships no ability to silently alter its data behavior, because it ships no update channel other than the releases you explicitly install.

One sentence version. Your words never leave your phone — not because a policy promises it, but because the app contains no code path that could.
